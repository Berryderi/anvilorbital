Does servicing beat burning up?

Years to fall, by starting altitude

Line: average density. Band: active sun to quiet sun. Dot: your fleet. Log scale.

Decay: drag on a circular orbit, using Vallado's exponential atmosphere table as copied in the open-source SatelliteToolbox. The sun is a flat x3 or x0.3 on density. Results run 1.8 to 4.3 times shorter than one published rough guide, and above 1,000 km the table is stretched past its last row. Trust above or below 5 years, not the exact number.

Other tools: NASA's free Debris Assessment Software checks compliance with NASA's debris requirements. This page adds cost and a servicing break-even for a whole fleet. It doesn't replace compliance analysis.

5-year limit: US and Canadian rules for new low-orbit licences. Older satellites can be exempt. Check your own licence.

Disposal: low-thrust fuel to reach 300 km, bought and launched. It excludes design and operations cost. It assumes the satellite still works. Failed satellites can't do this.

Burning up: 30 kg of aluminum oxide per 250 kg satellite, from Ferreira et al. 2024 (a molecular-dynamics model), scaled by mass. Real satellites differ. Other research suggests effects on ozone could differ, so this is an estimate of mass released, not of harm.

Servicing: it wins when its price is at or below the disposal it replaces plus the value recovered. Service prices and recovered value are your inputs. We found nobody offering this service for these fleets today, and no buyer for recovered parts.
